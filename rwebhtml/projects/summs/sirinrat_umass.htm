Description: (up to ~ 500 words)

Galaxies are thought to form and reside in extended cold dark matter halos. One of the ultimate challenges in astrophysics is therefore to obtain a detailed understanding of how galaxies with different physical properties occupy dark matter haloes of different mass. This relationship conveys important information about how different galaxies form and evolve in different dark matter haloes.

During the past 5 years, Houjun Mo at UMass and his collaborators (Xiaohu Yang and Frank van den Bosch) have embarked on a reasearch program to understand how galaxies form in dark matter halos as represented by galaxy groups selected from large redshift surveys. To achieve this, they have developed a halo-based group finder that is optimized for grouping galaxies that reside in the same dark matter halo. Using mock galaxy redshift surveys, it is found that this group finder is very successful in associating galaxies according to their common dark matter haloes. In particular, the group finder also performs reliably for poor groups and isolated galaxies in small mass halos. This makes this halo-based group finder ideally suited to study the relation between galaxies and dark matter halos over a wide dynamic range in halo masses.

Thus far, the halo-based group finder has been applied to both the 2dFGRS and to the SDSS-DR7. In these catalogs, each galaxy is assigned to a dark halo with a given halo mass allowing for an analysis of galaxy properties as a function of host halo mass and halo-centric distance, as well the average color of galaxies and the color of the central galaxy in the halo. These catalogs have been used to study how the stellar properties of galaxies (stellar mass, color, star formation rate) are affected by their environments as represented by their host halos. One of the pieces that is still missing is how the gaseous properties of galaxies are affected by their environments.

Clearly, with a large sample of HI-selected galaxies in the SDSS region, such as the one to be provided by ALFALFA survey, one can carry out a systematic analysis of how the gas properties of galaxies change with halo properties and with their locations in host halos. We, Jessica Rosenberg and Houjun Mo (from UMass, Amherst), propose a collaborative project using the ALFALFA and SDSS data along with the halo finding algorithm to study how gas properties change as the properties of the associated halo or position within the halo change.

This project will make use of the combined ALFALFA-SDSS data set that is being assembled by Jessica Rosenberg.With the merged ALFALFA-SDSS data and the information provided on the relative completeness of these two samples, we will be able to address the science as discussed above.

This project will involve one graduate student from the UMass,Sirinrat Sithajan, who has just started her second-year project and will likely continue to work on the topic for her PhD. We therefore envision a systematic analysis of the data and a thorough theoretical study about the implication for galaxy formation and evolution. Although the student will concentrate primarily on the study of galaxy properties and their dependence on halo properties, she will also help Jessica in constructing the value-added merged ALFALFA-SDSS lists.
