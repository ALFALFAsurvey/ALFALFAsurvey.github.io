  • ALFALFA Scheduling

    • The objective of ALFALFA scheduling during each observing season is to map completely, in 2 pass mode, two separate ALFALFA tiles.

    The "Spring" season focuses on observations in the R.A. range from 07h30m to 16h30m.

    The "Fall" season focuses on observations in the R.A. range from 22h to 3h.

    Given the complexities of the Arecibo telescope schedules in practice, we have mapped out a detailed plan of target drift(s) for each given observing session which tries to optimize the allocation to ALFALFA in two ways:
    • To maximize RA coverage over a large area contiguous in Declination and which overlaps with the SDSS DR3 dataset.
    • To insure that the 2nd pass is offset from the first by 3 months or more so that we can use the Doppler shift of the Earth's motion around the Sun to discriminate cosmic signals from RFI and spurious ones.

    All of the declination settings (corresponding to Beam 0 of the ALFA) for the entire ALFALFA survey have been preset. Contiguous drifts in the 1st pass are offset from each other by 14.6'. As part of the two-pass strategy, the 2nd pass drifts are offset from the 1st by 7.3' in order to even out the scalloping.

    We have planned out the first two months of observing, as summarized in the figures below. In the figures, the left hand label refers to the Beam 0 declination designation as given in the master list. The region highlighted in yellow indicates the range of RA of the time allocation of a given day, denoted by block yy.mm.dd as given in the block allocation summary. See the AO telescope schedules below for details. Note that we have only scheduled the first pass observations; the second pass will be added (in gold) when the Apr-Jul schedules are available.

    Note that this is the plan. Reality may be something quite different depending on hardware reliability and possible schedule changes. We do maintain a crude summary of completed observations, but it is not intended to be fully accurate.
