    • The EGGGEN utilities:    
      1. Learn how to start up IDL accessing the Arecibo and ALFALFA utilities. For this, you'll have to ask one of the local experts.
      2. "plotalfa"
        "plotalfa" allows you to explore how ALFA scans accross a region of the sky using the ALFALFA observing mode. In IDL, it is developed as a widget which allows for more flexibility in specifying the region plotted. You can elect to see the galaxies and ALFA beams/tracks in a square box of variable size from 30 arcmin (0.5 degrees) to 10 degrees with allowed values of 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0 ...10.0 . The bigger the region, the more galaxies that are plotted.
        • Investigate the 10h13m45s,+03d24'00" field. Here, enter the coordinates ("AGC style") and specify the box size to be 0.5 degrees; this is the same as "plot30".
        • How many galaxies do you find?
        • As you increase the box size, see how the number and distribution of galaxies change. What do you notice about the distribution of galaxies? How might this be explained?
        • Set the box size to 5 degrees. How many objects do you find?

      6. Some galaxies have two velocity measurements: "VOPT" and "V21". In that case, what should you adopt as the recessional velocity for the galaxy?

    • Numerical integration of an HI profile
      1. Use our links to find the ALFALFA data entry for AGC/UGC 7192. Put the link on your webpage.
      2. On your webpage, record for yourself the ALFALFA catalog information for this object. Do you understand what the parameters mean? Add notes for your own reference, as needed. If possible, enter the data as a table on your webpage; if you don't know how to do it, ask for a hint.
      3. Make a link on your web page to the journal article containing the ALFALFA publication of this spectrum.
      4. Download the spectrum (ASCII file format) to your disk area on hatillo.
      5. Write an IDL routine to calculate the integral under the profile. If you run into "issues", ask! This will probably take you some time as you need to know how to read in a file, among other things.

    • Reading ALFALFA catalogs:     ALFALFA produces catalogs containing a source name, the AGC number (where there is one), the HI centroid and the position of the optical counterpart, and then various parameters measured from the HI line.

      2. Write yourself a short IDL routine to read this test catalog, and try to calculate the HI mass from the parameter SintP

    • Tracing Galaxy Interactions with HI: Science Background
      Learn about galaxy-galaxy interactions and what HI can teach us about them. In other words, do some reading!
      1. Schweizer, F., 2000, "Interactions as a Driver of Galaxy Evolution", Philosophical Transations of the Royal Society of London, Series A, Vol. 358, no. 1772, pp. 2063-2076, (retrieve paper from astro-ph archive).
      2. Toomre, A. & Toomre, J. 1972, "Galactic Bridges and Tails", Astrophys. J. 178, 623.
      3. Barnes, J.E. & Hernquist, L., 1992, "Formation of Dwarf Galaxies in Tidal Tails", Nature, 360, pp. 715-717.
      4. Moore et al., 1995, "Galaxy Harassment and the Evolution of Clusters of Galaxies", Nature 379, 613, (retrieve paper from astro-ph archive)
      5. Hibbard, J.E. et al 2001 "High-Resolution H I Mapping of NGC 4038/39 ("The Antennae") and Its Tidal Dwarf Galaxy Candidates", Astron. J. 122, 2969.

      6. Sabrina's map of the Leo Triplet can be found as Figure 4 of Stierwalt et al 2009 Astron. J. 138 338. You may also want to take a look at the other papers about the Leo Triplet which are referenced in that paper.

        And check out some cool links:
      7. John Hibbard's HI Rogues' Gallery
