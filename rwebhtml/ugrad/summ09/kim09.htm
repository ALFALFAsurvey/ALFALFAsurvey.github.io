    Kim's project for the RCPRS program focuses on examining the properties and distribution of the galaxies with highest HI mass in the ALFALFA sample. In this respect, Kim's project overlaps with what Shan is doing, so Kim can use some of Shan's routines on how to calculate stellar masses etc. Kim's part will focus on SDSS and will look at what we can learn from colors and SDSS SFR indicators. Kim will also participate in some of the ALFALFA observations during the period August 14-21.

    • Here are some references relevant to using SDSS data to derive stellar masses. Read and study these papers as needed.
      ADS link Description
      Baldry et al 2004 astro-ph/0410603 Color bimodality: Implications for galaxy evolution, a conference proceedings paper reviewing SDSS results
      Baldry et al 2004 ApJ 600, 681 Quantifying the Bimodal Color-Magnitude Distribution of Galaxies, the longer paper on which the one above is based
      Miller et al 2003 ApJ 597, 142 The Environment of Active Galactic Nuclei in the Sloan Digital Sky Survey, analyzing the observed AGN fraction of AGN as a function of environment based on early SDSS results
      Hopkins et al 2003 ApJ 599, 971 Star Formation Rate Indicators in the Sloan Digital Sky Survey, describing methods of deriving the SFR from available SDSS data
      Kewley et al 2006 MNRAS 372, 961 The host galaxies and classification of active galactic nuclei, more discussion of SDSS spectroscopic diagnostics
      Schlegel, Finkbeiner & Davis 1998 ApJ 500, 525 Maps of Dust Infrared Emission for Use in Estimation of Reddening and Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation Foregrounds, including conversions to calculate extinctions in different wavelength/filter bands.
      Blanton et al. 2003 AJ 125, 2348 Estimating Fixed-Frame Galaxy Magnitudes in the Sloan Digital Sky Survey.
      Giovanelli et al 1995 AJ 110, 1059 Dependence on Luminosity of Photometric Properties of Disk Galaxies: Surface Brightness, Size, and Internal Extinction giving internal extinction correction, according to axial ratio and luminosity, for I-band
      Fukugita, Shimasaku, & Ichikawa, T. 1995 PASP 107, 945 Galaxy Colors in Various Photometric Band Systems, including conversion relations for different filter systems
      Bell et al 2003 ApJS 149, 289 The Optical and Near-Infrared Properties of Galaxies. I. Luminosity and Stellar Mass Functions, including appendix with relationship between M/L ratios and stellar masses. See also the ALFALFA U-grad page

    • Think about the SCIENCE questions: What are the massive HI galaxies found by ALFALFA? Are they just the high mass end of the distribution in all senses, i.e. "big galaxies are big in all ways"? => Are they also the most luminous? Do they have the same color distribution as the rest of the ALFALFA galaxies? Are they forming stars at the same rate as the other galaxies in ALFALFA? How do they compare to a magnitude-limited sample of SDSS galaxies? Where are they located and how does their distribution within the large scale structure compare to other bright galaxies found in SDSS? Are subsets of them unusual in any way? How do they fit into the general picture of "downsizing"? Note: You'll have to do some reading!

    • Update your sample of massive galaxies using the newest ALFALFA catalog. Here is the current work-in-progress catalog. Note: this catalog isn't perfect; there may be errors/duplicates. BEWARE!
      We need to decide/remind ourselves how to define your sample in a way that makes it complete to some HI mass limit. Whatever code you write should be flexible enough that you can run it on a whole ALFALFA catalog and then pick out the most massive objects. Shan has worked on identifying galaxies with log M(HI)> 10.0, and she can certainly help you; you may want to be more restrictive and look closely at a smaller sample. Things are trickier than they might appear; for example, to derive distances, you need to correct the observed redshift from the heliocentric rest frame. There are EGG routines to do this! So think about how to proceed, and we can discuss how to proceed.
      1. Take a look at the routines in /home/dorado3/galaxy/eggidl/gen and the associated documentation files; you can find some documentation here but if may not be up to date.
      2. A year ago, we made up cross tables matching SDSS with the ALFALFA catalog we had at that time. The files are in /home/dorado3/galaxy/sdsscross. See what's there. Look at the two files that Shan and Ann made up: spectroscopic.html and photometric.html. Before you can perform the cross-match with all the ALFALFA galaxies, we need the gradEGGs to update the cross files. For the moment, you can focus on developing the methodology for the objects which are in the 2008 cross files.
      3. Derive HI masses for all the code 1 objects in the ALFALFA catalog. You'll need distances, so you need to learn how to run the flowmodel code; and of course, you'll also need to understand what the flowmodel does! Then Make a histogram of HI masses for all the objects in the master catalog (above).
      4. Match your catalog of HI masses (all masses, not just the high mass ones) with the 2008 version of the cross match. It is an IDL structure called /home/dorado3/galaxy/sdsscross/agcsdss.sav. You can also experiement with alfalfa_crosstable_jun2008.sav.
      5. Discuss with Shan how to calculate absolute magnitudes (R band) and corrected colors. What do you have to make corrections for? Write an explanation of everything that has to be done and how you will go about doing it.
      6. Then try to make a color-magnitude diagram similar to Figure 9 of Baldry et al 2004 (see reference above). Do all our galaxies fit onto their plot? Note: Shan has done something similar but you should produce your own version. She can help you to use some of her code, but be sure you understand every step along the way. Write it all down for me.
      7. Then color code the galaxies by bin of HI mass, or alternatively, make a set of plots for separate bins of HI mass. What do you find? Also make plots of HI mass versus absolute magnitude and HI mass versus color. Be sure to use logarithmic units where that makes sense.
      8. Think about what else to do. Write it all down......
