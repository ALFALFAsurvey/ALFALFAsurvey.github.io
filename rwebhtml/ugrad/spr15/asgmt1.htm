3. &nsbp Apparent magnitude and surface brightness
We often find it convenient to talk about a galaxy's surface brightness, expressed in units of magnitudes per square arc second (arcsec). Suppose that we can describe a galaxy as a thin disk of area A where A is πR2 for a face-on galaxy of radius R or π(a × b)/4 for an inclined disk of major-to-minor diameter axial ratio a/b. Let L/A represent the galaxy luminosity divided by the area. What we observe is the galaxy's surface brightness μ = -2.5 log (L/A). Show that the observed surface brightness μ can be related to the apparent magnitude by
        μ = m + -2.5 log (A/d2)
where d is the distance to the galaxy. Furthermore, show that 2.5 log (π/4) = 0.26, so that, for diameters measured in arcsec ("), the expression becomes
        μ = m + -2.5 log (a" x b") + 0.26
