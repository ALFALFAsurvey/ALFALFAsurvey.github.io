Tidal Dwarfs and Tails

Sarah Higdon (Georgia Southern University/Cornell University) & James Higdon (Georgia Southern University)

In addition to triggering starbursts and active galactic nuclei the mergers of disk galaxies frequently lead to the formation of tidal tails. The formation and evolution of Tidal Dwarf Galaxies (TDGs), within these tails is poorly understood, due in part to the limitations of available observations. Dwarf galaxies are the most common galaxy type in the current epoch and TDGs may make up 1/3 of the dwarf population in compact groups. The Spitzer Space Telescope has made mid-infrared studies of these faint objects possible. This poster uses mid-infrared diagnostic tools to characterize the star forming environments in a sample of TDGs. Some are undergoing intense star formation and are rich in PAH molecules.

GSU has recently become involved in the Arecibo Legacy Fast ALFA (ALFALFA) undergraduate research program. We will use part of the poster to advertise this great opportunity! GSU students have already participated in an observing run and will be searching for tidal tails this summer. Any found without visible perturbers are potential candidates for small dark matter halo interactions.
