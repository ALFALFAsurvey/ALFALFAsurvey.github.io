The ALFALFA View of the Local HI Universe

Ann Martin (Cornell) and the ALFALFA Consortium

The ALFALFA blind HI survey will enable a a census of the distribution of gas-rich galaxies in the local Universe. Sensitive to an HI mass of 107 solar masses at the distance of the Virgo cluster, ALFALFA will probe the smallest objects locally and provide a new consideration of the "missing satellites" problem, while also covering enough volume to explore the dependence of galaxy formation on environment. Additionally, with a larger, cosmologically significant sample volume and wider bandwidth than previous blind surveys (e.g. HIJASS, HIPASS), a much larger number of detections in each mass bin is possible, with adequate angular resolution to eliminate the need for extensive follow-up observations. For instance, the first catalog includes 730 sources, of which 69% are new HI detections, compared to the HIPASS survey of the same region which detected only 40 signals. This increased sensitivity will greatly enhance the utility of cosmological probes in HI. ALFALFA will measure the correlation function of HI selected galaxies in a large local volume, and will be able to identify the bias present in correlation functions of HI- and optically-selected objects. The larger sample and volume size of the ALFALFA dataset will also robustly measure the HI mass function (HIMF), particularly the faint-end slope, where current measurements disagree by an order of magnitude near 106 solar masses. Here, we present the preliminary ALFALFA results on the distribution of local gas-rich galaxies from the first year of data reduction and catalog production.
