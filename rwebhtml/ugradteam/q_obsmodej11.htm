Calculations

Three target drifts are listed for the Workshop:
  •    M 17Jan  134p1   +322906 from AST 01h45-06h30
  •    T 18Jan  138p1   +332730 from AST 01h30-06h15
  •    W 19Jan  136p1   +325818 from AST 01h30-06h15
Let's look at drift 134p1, at declination +322906, to be observed from AST0145-0630, i.e., 1:45 - 6:30AM Arecibo time, on Jan 17.
Detailed instructions courtesy Catherine

  1. For this drift, list the resultant seven declinations covered by ALFA's seven beams, in order, from south to north. Visit this page and this memo for help. Note the central beam (designated Beam "0") is the one that is set to the declination listed.
    The central beam (beam 0) is the starting point for this calculation. The declination for beam 0 is given in the observing schedule. Beam 1 is the most southern and beam 4 is the most northern. This is based on the central beam being above +18d, indicating that the dec. range is north of the zenith. The offset between the beams is 2.1 arcmin (2 arcmin 6 arcsec). By taking beam 0 and adding 2.1 arcmin the resultant declination is projected for beam 5. From there you would add 2.1 arcmin to the results for beam 5 to get the declination for 3 and then add 2.1 to the declination of beam 3 to get the declination at beam 4. To get the more southern declinations start from beam 0 and subtract 2.1 arcmin from beam 0 to get declination at beam 2 then subtract 2.1 arcmin from that to get declination at beam 6 and then once more for the declination from beam 1.

  2. What is the resultant declination range covered by the 7 beams of ALFA for this drift? (Hint: consider the full width of each beam, the figures on this page are helpful.)
    The declination range is basically accounted for based on the results obtained from question 1. All that needs to be taken into account to get the full range is the ends of beams 1 and 4. Looking at the graphs from the memo ALFA Beam Configurations on the Sky one can see that to account for the ends of beams 1 and 4, 2.1 arcmins need to added to the declination of beam 4 and subtract 2.1 arcmins from the declination of beam 1.

  3. What is the range in local sidereal time (LST) over the course of the run? This is approximately the right ascension range that we will cover. (Hint: look again at the summary of upcoming observing page.)
    The LST (local sidereal time) range which is approximately the RA range that will be covered. There are two ways in which we can calculate this.
    Method 1 is using the program blocksched.pro from IDL. The command for this isblockshed, yymmdd, ast1HHMM, ast2HHMM. For the first drift we would enter into the command line: blockshed,100117L,0145L,0630L .
    Method 2 has the initial starting point given to us by Martha in summary of upcoming observing . Using this reference point the next two LST ranges can be calculated. The starting time (AST in the observing schedule) is 15 minutes earlier for the second drift compared with the first drift. This difference must be accounted for when calculating the range. The starting time stays the same for the third drift compared with the second drift and therefore a difference is not accounted for. For each day the LST differs by 4mins and must be accounted for in the LST range calculations (through addition).

  4. What is the zenith angle of this drift as observed with ALFA beam 0?
    The zenith angle is calculated by taking the declination value at beam 0 and subtracting it from the latitude line of Arecibo (+18d21).

  5. At what azimuth will we orient the azimuth arm so that ALFA can observe this declination strip?
    First answer: is this declination north or south of the zenith at Arecibo? Then look at the explanation on the ALFALFA Observing Mode page to figure out the answer.

  6. What are the redshift and velocity ranges covered by ALFALFA?
    Check out the table at the very bottom of the ALFALFA Science page .
