The Search for Galaxies in the Pisces-Perseus Void
A challenge to current cosmological models of structure formation is the apparent discrepancy between the number of observed galaxies in voids and the number of low mass dark matter halos predicted by simulations to reside in them. In this project Alison will investigate the distribution of galaxies in the region of the Pisces-Perseus supercluster (PPS) and its foreground void which is one of the most prominent "empty" structures in the local universe. Alison will use public databases as well as the ALFALFA catalog and ALFALFA team followup observations to define the void (with the help of grad student Mike Jones) and the search for galaxies within it.

Goals for this semester include:
  • To understand the cosmological significance of the "Void Phenomenon"
  • To understand the large scale structure in and around the PPS and its foreground void and the methods used to define that structure.
  • To analyze the L-band wide spectral data taken in Nov 13 to extract the HI properties of detections
  • To become sufficiently familiar with IDL that I understand the routines that fit baselines and measure RMS values ("lbwbaseline") and extract the HI parameters ("lbwmeasure") so that I can contribute to making them more robust and useful to the ALFALFA team.
  • To begin learning PYTHON.


A CSV file with interesting data
Here is a CSV file for your use. It contains all the entries of known redshift (recessional velocity, cz) in our AGC database as of 11March2014 with the following selection criteria: (1) 315 deg < R.A. < 50 deg; (2) -1 deg < Dec. < +50 deg; (3) +200 km/s < Vhelio < +18000 km/s (where the recessional velocity comes from any source, optical or radio). Columns are as follows.
  1. AGCnr:     Identification number in the AGC (our database)
  2. radeg:     Right ascension (J2000.0) in decimal degrees
  3. ppsradeg:     Right ascension (J2000.0) in decimal degrees, except wrapped around so that if R.A. > 315., ppsradeg = radeg-360.0
  4. decdeg:     Declination (J2000.0) in decimal degrees
  5. a:    major diameter of galaxy, in arcminutes (where available)
  6. b:     minor diameter of galaxy, in arcminutes (where available)
  7. zmag:     optical magnitude (where available; not very precise)
  8. vopt:     recessional velocity from optical spectrum in km/s (where available)
  9. hiflux:     HI line flux density in Jy-km/s (where available)
  10. rms:     rms noise in the HI line spectrum in mJy/channel (where available)
  11. v21:     recessional velocity from HI spectrum in km/s (where available)
  12. width:     width of the HI line in km/s (where available)
  13. widtherr:     error on width in km/s (where available)
  14. snr:     signal-to-noise ratio of the HI line emission (where available)
  15. hisrc:     code referring to the source of the HI spectrum
  16. vhelagc:     heliocentric recessional velocity in km/s either from vopt or v21 (v21 preferred if available and good quality)

  • A color magnitude diagram of SDSS galaxies in the Coma supercluster
    A common way to explore the properties of a population of galaxies involves constructing its color-magnitude diagram (CMD). A nice example which we will use for reference is the work of ALFALFA team member Peppo Gavazzi and his co-workers who constructed the CMD of a large sample of galaxies within 420 square degrees of sky covering the Coma supercluster and its member groups and clusters of galaxies as presented in Gavazzi et al (2010), A&A 517, 73 and shown here to the right. Taken from Fig. 3 of that paper, the figure shows the "g-i color versus i-band absolute magnitude relation of all galaxies in the C[oma]S[upercluster] coded according to Hubble type: red = early- type galaxies (dE-E-S0-S0a); blue = disk galaxies (Sbc-Im-BCD); green = bulge galaxies (Sa-Sb)... Contours of equal density are given. The continuum line g-i = -0.0585 *(Mi + 16) + 0.78 represents the empirical separation between the red-sequence and the remaining galaxies. The dashed line illustrates the effect of the limiting magnitude r=17.77 of the spectroscopic SDSS database, combined with the color of the faintest E galaxies g-i ~0.70 mag.."

    Be sure you understand this diagram and what it shows.
