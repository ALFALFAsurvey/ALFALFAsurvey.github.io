The ALFALFA HI line detection catalog is based on a source identification and measurement scheme intended to deliver a homogeneous and well understood dataset of HI line detections. HI line sources with a signal-to-noise ratio (S/N) of greater than 6.5 are deemed to be highest quality detections, and some (but not all) of the lower S/N sources may also be real. We always have to worry that our data are corrupted by man-made radio transmissions or, in the case of low level signals, are just not statistically distinguishable from radio noise.

As part of the catalog generation process, optical images from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS; where available) and other public imaging databases are examined and the "most probable" optical counterpart is identified. What we are particularly interested is the possible existence of gas-bearing "dark galaxies", HI sources with no stellar counterparts. The best example of "dark galaxy" known so far is the southwest component of HI1225+01;(the dark galaxy then is known as HI1225+01b).
